How to read a score

A headline, and a separate note.

Three cues sit beside the draft. Only one of them is the percentage in the center of the pie.

AI-like

The number on the gauge. Only lines classified as AI-like. Inconclusive lines are not added in.

Inconclusive

A separate note beside the headline. Purple highlights are a reason to reread, not part of the AI-like score.

Confidence

Lower on short text, on mixed sections, and on writing that may not be English. Under 300 words, treat the result as a sketch.

Questions

Before a percentage stands in for a decision.

Does a high score prove a chatbot wrote the text?

No. The number is the share classified as AI-like by one English model. It is a reason to reread a passage. It is not a finding of misconduct, and it does not grade the work.

What is saved, and what stays on this page?

Pasted text and a rewrite stay in the browser unless you are looking at them now. A PDF that produces a report can be kept with your account after you sign in. You can delete that report from the dashboard.

Where does account data live?

Your name, email, and saved PDF reports are stored on the computer that runs Flyleaf. A password is stored only as a hash. If you use Google, Google confirms the email and Flyleaf keeps the link to that account. See the privacy note.

Will a rewrite hide the AI-like lines?

No. Humanize is a plainer draft for you to review. The meaning should stay. A later check can still mark the result. Flyleaf does not promise a lower score.

What does Similarity check?

Similarity looks for close matches on public web pages found for this draft, then builds a short PDF report. It does not search publication libraries or student-paper databases. A match is a reason to cite or rewrite. It is not a finding of misconduct.
